01

The complete journey

16 steps

Every feature in this document is a detailed view of one of these steps. The order is real — each step depends on the one before it, which is why the schedule is numbered.

  1. Customer logs in.

    Compulsory — construction is a protected service. No introduction screens; they land on their dashboard.

  2. They open Construction Services.

    A list of the kinds of work available, each with a short description of what it covers.

  3. They fill in an enquiry.

    What work, where the site is, roughly how big, what they can spend, when they want it done. Photographs can be attached.

  4. The system confirms it.

    A reference number goes back immediately, so nothing gets lost.

  5. Verified contractors are matched.

    By type of work, location and availability. The customer can inspect each profile.

  6. A site visit is arranged.

    The contractor sees the site, records measurements and conditions, takes photographs — in the app, not on paper.

  7. The contractor prepares a quote.

    An itemised list: each item of work, the quantity, the rate, the amount, plus terms and exclusions.

  8. The customer reviews it.

    Compares against other quotes, asks questions, requests changes.

  9. The customer accepts a quote.

    The turning point. The enquiry becomes a live project, and the agreed price and scope are locked.

  10. The work is divided into stages.

    Each with a description, a target date, and how much of the total it is worth.

  11. The customer funds the project.

    Money goes in and is held. The contractor can see it is real but cannot touch it.

  12. The contractor completes a stage.

    Dated photographs are uploaded as proof.

  13. The stage is approved.

    The customer — or a supervisor — confirms the work is genuinely done.

  14. That stage's payment is released.

    Automatically. Nobody has to chase anybody.

  15. Steps 12–14 repeat.

    Stage by stage, until the work is finished.

  16. The project is handed over.

    Final approval, final payment, all papers stored, and the customer rates the contractor.

Why steps 11 to 14 are the whole design

The customer's money goes in before work starts, so the contractor knows it is real. But it is released only as each stage is genuinely completed and approved. The customer never pays for work not done; the contractor never works without knowing the money exists. That single mechanism is what makes the module trustworthy — everything else is built around it.

05

How money is handled

7 steps · foundation built

Where most of the module's value sits, and where mistakes would be most expensive. The mechanism below is already built and tested — it is the only part of the module that exists in code today.

  1. The customer puts money into the app.

    It sits in their wallet, marked as reserved for this project.

  2. The money is held, not transferred.

    The contractor can see it is there and knows it is real, but cannot take it. It also cannot be spent on food, a taxi or anything else.

  3. The contractor completes a stage.

    Dated photographs are uploaded as proof.

  4. The stage is approved.

    By the customer, or by a supervisor, depending on the setting you choose.

  5. Only that stage's money is released.

    The rest stays held. This is the one transition that turns held money into someone else's money.

  6. This repeats stage by stage.

    Until the work is done.

  7. A final portion may be held back.

    For an agreed period after completion, in case defects appear.

What is already working

An append-only escrow ledger backs every one of those steps. A hold never moves money — it marks part of the balance as spoken for, and the shared payment path across all six existing modules now refuses to spend it. Every release carries a stable reference, so a retried or double-tapped approval pays the contractor exactly once.

Three guarantees hold at all times: held = released + refunded + outstanding; no release without an approved stage; and no ledger row can ever be edited or deleted — corrections are new entries, and the original stays visible forever.

06

The rules the business has set

6 platform rules

These come from the main platform blueprint and are already agreed. They apply to construction whether or not construction is mentioned by name, and they shape what the module can and cannot do.

Rule 01

One account for everything

A person has exactly one account across the whole app. A contractor who is also a customer uses one account for both.

ALREADY TRUE
Rule 02

All money goes through the app's wallet

Every rupee moves through one central wallet system. Construction cannot build its own separate payment arrangement.

BUILT · P0
Rule 03

Financial records can never be changed

To correct a mistake, a new correcting entry is added — the original stays visible forever. The escrow ledger enforces this absolutely; wallet balances remain a reconciled cache.

BUILT · P0
Rule 04

Every important action is recorded

When a quote is sent, a stage approved, or money released, the system records who did it, when, and what changed. These records cannot be edited or deleted.

BUILT · P0
Rule 05

Construction is a protected service

The user must be logged in and verified before they can use anything at all. Construction involves large sums and access to people's homes — anonymous use is not acceptable.

PHASE 2
Rule 06

Contractors verified before receiving work

A contractor cannot receive a single enquiry until your team has checked their documents. The system tracks expiry dates and warns when a licence is about to lapse.

PHASE 3

One rule in the BRD does not match the code

The BRD warns that a contractor accepting a six-month project would be locked out of all other work for six months, and calls this a blocking question. The code does not do this. The existing rule limits a provider's catalogue to one category; it does not trigger on job acceptance, and approved providers can change their selection at any time. No provider is ever blocked by an in-flight job.

Contractors get their own profile type and are not subject to that rule at all. Instead they declare multiple trades and a maximum concurrent project count (W2), which is the honest version of the same idea. This question needs no platform change.

Choosing needs browse, compare and decide screens; assigning needs an accept-or-reject screen. Our recommendation — the app shortlists, the customer chooses — is what the design assumes. PHASE 4

Q18 Should a finished project become an AMC customer automatically? Cross-module integration with your Property Care module. An obvious source of repeat revenue, but it was never stated as a requirement. CONFIRM

Two of these are worth answering this week

Q8 gates the entire payment model. The mechanism is built and tested, but it must not go live against real customer money without your written confirmation that you intend to hold funds and on what basis.

Q10 is cheap to answer now and expensive later. If sub-contractor payments are wanted, the escrow layer needs to branch rather than run in a straight line — and that is a foundation change, not a feature addition.
