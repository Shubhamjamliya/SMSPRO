01

The eight that were blocking

Q1–Q8

Six of these are now answered — either by reading the code, or by making the decision a setting you control rather than something we hardcode. Two still need you.

Q1

Is construction a full project system, or just another service booking?

SETTLED
Our answerA full project system. Not a judgement call — we checked the code. Every model in the platform is a short-lived order, ride or booking. Nothing models a job that runs for months in stages.
WhyA food order lives for 40 minutes and has one payment. A house lives for a year and has ten. Bolting construction onto the booking pattern would mean rewriting it within six months.
CostThis is why the module is 18 data models and 8 build phases rather than 3 and 2. It is the single largest driver of timeline.
Q2

How do we handle the one-category-at-a-time rule for long projects?

NO CHANGE NEEDED
Our answerNothing needs to change. The problem described in the BRD does not exist in the code. The existing rule limits a provider's catalogue to one category. It does not trigger when a job is accepted, and approved providers can change their selection at any time. No provider is ever locked by an in-flight job.
InsteadContractors get their own profile type and are not subject to that rule at all. They declare multiple trades and a maximum number of concurrent projects — which is the honest version of the same idea.
CostZero. This was flagged as a blocker; it is not one.
Q3

What construction services will you actually offer?

YOUR LIST
Our answerYou fill this in yourself, through the admin screen. We are not hardcoding it. A starter list to design against: New Building Construction · Renovation & Remodelling · Interior Fit-out · Structural Repair & Waterproofing · Extension or Additional Floor · Commercial & Shop Fit-out · Boundary Wall & External Works · Turnkey House
WhyYour service list will change often, and it should never need a software release. This was already a requirement in its own right (A8).
We needCorrect or replace the eight above. Category and sub-category names are enough — descriptions can come later.
Q4

Does the customer choose the contractor, or does the app assign one?

CONFIRM BY PHASE 4
Our answerThe app shortlists three to five verified contractors; the customer chooses from that shortlist. This matches the BRD's own recommendation, and it is what the design currently assumes.
WhyAssignment works for a plumber because the job is commoditised and cheap. A house is neither. Being able to compare and choose is the main reason a customer uses a platform rather than asking a relative.
OtherwisePure assignment removes the browse, profile, filter and compare screens — roughly a week less work, but it also removes most of what makes the module worth using.
Q5

Can several contractors quote for the same enquiry?

A SETTING
Our answerYes — capped at three, and adjustable. Uncapped competition means contractors spend days on site visits they mostly lose, and they stop responding. Three is enough to make comparison meaningful without burning your supply side.
WhySide-by-side quote comparison is one of the strongest features in the module. It only exists if more than one contractor can quote.
CostNone — it is already a configurable number. Set it to 1 and comparison simply disappears.
Q6

What exactly goes into a quotation?

SEND ONE SAMPLE
Our answerSections, then line items: description, quantity, unit, rate, amount. Then subtotal, tax, total, validity period, inclusions, exclusions, and the proposed payment stages. Line-item columns and standard terms are admin-editable, so you can reshape the quote without a release.
We needOne real quotation in the format you want. That single document is worth more than a page of written answers — it settles column names, tax presentation and terms in one go.
Q7

Who decides the stages and their payment percentages?

A SETTING
Our answerThe contractor proposes them in the quote, within limits you set. Suggested guardrails: between 3 and 12 stages · no single stage worth more than 40% · first stage no more than 20% · final stage at least 10%.
WhyStage structure genuinely differs between a bathroom renovation and a two-floor build, so a fixed platform template would be wrong half the time. The guardrails are what stop a contractor writing "70% on mobilisation" — which is the abuse the staged model exists to prevent.
OtherwiseYou can switch to platform-fixed templates per service type, or full per-project negotiation. Both are supported; the flexible middle is the one we would ship first.
Q8

Confirm the money-holding model and what releases the funds.

BLOCKING
Our answerThe mechanism is built and tested. The legal position is yours to confirm, in writing, before it touches real money. Money is held in the customer's own wallet and marked reserved — it never moves to a platform account until a stage is approved. That distinction may matter a great deal to your legal advice.
Release triggerCustomer approval of a photographed, dated stage. See Q13 for what happens when a customer simply does not respond.
If holding is not permittedTwo fallbacks: route funds through a licensed escrow provider (a different integration, roughly two weeks), or drop holding entirely and use the platform only to confirm milestones while the customer pays the contractor directly. The second is much weaker — it gives up the guarantee that makes the module trustworthy.
We needWritten confirmation that you intend to hold customer funds, and on what legal basis. Not a verbal yes.
02

Important, but not blocking

Q9–Q16

These shape the product rather than stop it. Q10 is the exception — it is here in the BRD's ordering, but it affects code that already exists, so it should be answered now.

Q9

Can contractors be companies with several staff logins?

LATER
Our answerDesign it in now, build it later. One owner login at launch. The project record already carries a participant list with separate permissions, so adding staff logins later needs no migration and breaks nothing.
WhyMulti-user contractor firms need their own invite, role and permission screens — a meaningful amount of work for something most contractors will not use in the first year. Build it when a firm actually asks.
Q10

Can a main contractor bring in sub-contractors through the app?

ANSWER NOW
Our answerNo — the main contractor is solely responsible and pays their own specialists off-platform. If you disagree, say so this week rather than in two months.
Why it is urgentPaying sub-contractors through the app turns the money layer from a straight line into a branching tree: one held amount splitting to several payees, each needing its own approval, dispute path and tax treatment. That layer is already built and tested. Changing it later means rebuilding working code.
If you want itRoughly two extra weeks on the payment layer, plus a materially harder dispute model — when a sub-contractor is unpaid, who does the customer hold responsible? Our recommendation is to keep that question out of your platform.
Q11

Is the site visit free or charged?

A SETTING
Our answerFree at launch. Build the fee and its refund rule, but leave them switched off.
WhyA charge at the site-visit step is friction at exactly the moment you are still proving the platform works. Switch it on later, when customer demand outstrips contractor supply and no-shows start costing you.
Q12

How is GST shown on quotes and settlements?

NEEDS YOUR CA
Our answerTax shown exclusive — per line item and again in the summary. The contractor invoices the customer; the platform invoices the contractor for its commission. This treats you as a facilitator rather than the supplier of construction services, which is the lighter position.
We needYour accountant to confirm that invoicing direction. It changes who issues what document, and it is not a decision developers should make.
Q13

Who confirms a stage is genuinely complete?

A SETTING
Our answerThe customer approves. If they do not respond within seven days, it escalates to your supervisor, who can approve on the photographic evidence. This is the single most important fairness setting in the module.
WhyThe BRD raises this risk itself: if the customer alone can approve, a difficult customer can withhold a contractor's payment indefinitely for no good reason. Word of that spreads through a contractor network faster than anything else, and your supply side disappears.
OtherwiseCustomer-only approval is simpler and more appealing to customers, but it makes the platform unattractive to exactly the good contractors you most want. Supervisor-required is the opposite trade. The escalation model gets most of both.
Q14

What happens when the customer changes their mind mid-project?

BUILD IT · PHASE 5
Our answerVariation orders, built in Phase 5 rather than deferred. A variation is a small addendum to the original quote. Once the customer accepts it, the agreed price adjusts and stages are added or resized.
Why not laterScope change is not an edge case in construction, it is near-certain. Without a mechanism the agreed price cannot legitimately change — so both sides settle it over the phone and in cash, and your record of the project stops being true. That undermines every other guarantee the module makes.
Q15

What happens when the two sides disagree?

BUILD IT · PHASE 6
Our answerA simple dispute path, not an arbitration system. Either side raises a dispute on a stage; that stage's money freezes; your team reviews the evidence and resolves it by releasing, refunding, or splitting. Freezing is already supported by the payment layer — a disputed stage simply never reaches its release step.
WhyThere is no dispute process anywhere on the platform today. With projects worth lakhs, every disagreement otherwise becomes a phone call to your office with no record and no neutral position to stand on.
Keep it smallResist appeal tiers and formal arbitration. One review step by your team covers almost every real case.
Q16

What documents must the app store?

CONFIRM LIST
Our answerAgreement or contract · approved drawings · government approvals and permits · structural certificates · material bills · completion certificate · warranty documents · site photographs. The list is admin-editable, so add categories as you learn what people actually upload.
Why it mattersThese papers are needed years later — for resale, insurance or repairs — and most people lose them. Storing them well is quietly one of the module's most valuable features.

Q17

ASSUMED NO
Our answerNo — explicitly out of scope.
WhyEach is a module in its own right. Material supply in particular would roughly double the size of this one, and it brings inventory, delivery and returns with it. Better as Module 09 than as a bolt-on here.
Q18

Should a finished project automatically become a maintenance customer?

ASSUMED: OFFER, NOT AUTO
Our answerOffer it at handover. Do not enrol them automatically.
WhyAuto-enrolling someone into a paid plan they did not ask for is a trust problem, and probably a consent problem. Offering an AMC on the handover screen — right when they are happiest with the work — costs almost nothing to build and converts better anyway.
Q19

Do contractors use the same app as plumbers and electricians?

ALREADY ANSWERED
Our answerSame app, separate login and separate screens. This is exactly how your Service Provider module already works, so the pattern is proven rather than invented.
CostNo second app to build, publish or maintain. Contractors get an experience built for them without you carrying another release pipeline.
Q20

Should construction sites have their own QR code?

ASSUMED: LATER
Our answerA genuine fit, but not on the critical path. Revisit after launch.
WhyYou already have a QR Safety module, and a site QR that lets a contractor check in and shows site details would slot in naturally. It just does not earn its place ahead of quotes, stages or payments.
